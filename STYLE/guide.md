# Pranay's Technical Content Style Guide

## Core voice

- Direct and concise
- First person when expressing Pranay's experience or position
- No hype, fake urgency, or guru language
- Strongly contrarian when evidence warrants it
- Precise about what is known, measured, inferred, and unknown
- Advanced by default, with enough explanation for intermediate practitioners

## The reader experience

A strong post should make a technical reader think one of the following:

- “I understand this paper or method more accurately now.”
- “I can reproduce or apply this.”
- “I was overlooking an important limitation.”
- “This changed how I would design the experiment or system.”

## Openings

Start with one of these:

- A specific technical claim
- A result that conflicts with common belief
- A mistake or failed experiment
- A practical consequence of a paper or benchmark
- A precise question the post answers

Do not begin with throat-clearing such as “AI is changing rapidly,” “In today's world,” or “I recently came across.”

## Supported structures

### Concise insight

1. Claim or tension
2. Explanation
3. Concrete evidence/example
4. Takeaway or unresolved question

### Technical tutorial

1. Problem
2. Method or code
3. Result
4. Why it works
5. Caveat or failure mode
6. Practical next step

### Research breakdown

1. What the paper claims
2. What the method actually tests
3. The most important result
4. What the result does not prove
5. Practical implication
6. Sources

### Lesson story

1. Specific event or mistake
2. Initial assumption
3. Diagnosis
4. Evidence that changed the view
5. Practical lesson

## Contrarian content rules

- Attack weak assumptions, methods, incentives, or practices—not people.
- A strong claim must have stronger evidence than a neutral claim.
- State what evidence would change the conclusion.
- Include the strongest reasonable counterargument when space allows.
- Never manufacture controversy merely to increase replies.

## Evidence rules

- Include at least one concrete example or code snippet when applicable.
- Cite at least one credible source, benchmark, statistic, or reproducible result for external claims.
- Prefer primary sources.
- Clearly label personal interpretation.
- Mention meaningful limitations, uncertainty, and confounders.

## Attribution and tagging

- End with `Sources` containing 1–3 links.
- Verify official X and LinkedIn accounts before tagging.
- Tag a person or organization in the body only if their work is central and the attribution reads naturally.
- Where possible, list the source author's relevant verified profile beside the source.
- If the profile cannot be verified, omit the tag rather than guess.

## Platform formatting

### X

- Use a single post when the idea fits; do not force a thread.
- For a thread, one claim or step per post.
- No hashtags unless there is a specific event/community reason.
- Avoid unnecessary emojis.
- Put detailed sources in the final post or first reply when character limits require it.

### LinkedIn

- Use short paragraphs and meaningful whitespace.
- Technical depth is welcome; do not dilute into generic career advice.
- Use a diagram, code excerpt, or result when it materially clarifies the idea.
- End with a real technical question only when discussion would improve the post.
- No generic engagement bait.

### Monthly blog

- Expand a proven social idea rather than choosing an unrelated topic.
- Include code, figures, methods, assumptions, limitations, and references as appropriate.
- Optimize for durable usefulness, not a news-cycle spike.

## Excalidraw visual style

- Hand-drawn, whiteboard-like, and explanatory
- White or warm off-white background
- Dark charcoal strokes
- Limited pastel palette with consistent semantic colors
- Large readable labels; no decorative microtext
- One clear visual thesis
- Prefer flows, comparisons, architectures, experiment pipelines, and concept maps
- Include alt text

## Never do this

- Fabricate personal stories, benchmarks, code output, quotations, or citations
- Present a paper's abstract claim as an independently verified fact
- Hide limitations that change the interpretation
- Guess social handles
- Use generic motivation, corporate filler, or trend-chasing hype
- Copy another creator's angle or phrasing
