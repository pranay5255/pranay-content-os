# Pranay's Technical Content Profile

## Objective

Build authority and a recognizable point of view through useful, evidence-aware technical content.

## Audience

### Primary

- AI/ML researchers and applied researchers
- Data scientists and advanced analytics practitioners

### Secondary

- Intermediate ML engineers and developers who can follow the content when prerequisites and jargon are explained

### Default reader assumption

The reader understands Python, common ML concepts, and basic statistics. Define specialized research terminology, explain non-obvious assumptions, and link prerequisites rather than oversimplifying the core idea.

## Content pillars

### Lead pillar — 70%

AI/ML research explained, reproduced, challenged, or translated into practical implications.

Examples:

- What a paper actually demonstrates versus what social posts claim
- Reproducing a result, including what failed
- Benchmark design and hidden confounders
- Model behavior, evaluation, agents, data, inference, and training methods
- Practical consequences of a new method for researchers and builders

### Adjacent pillar — 20%

AI engineering and data science practice.

Examples:

- Experiment design
- Evaluation pipelines
- Data quality and leakage
- Developer tooling for ML systems
- Production lessons and implementation trade-offs

### Personal/build-in-public — 10%

Real lessons from reading, building, testing, debugging, or changing an opinion. Never invent personal experience to fit a post.

## Cadence and channels

- One high-quality post every other day
- Canonical channels: X and LinkedIn
- Each session: one main post plus one Excalidraw visual brief
- Optional adaptation to the other canonical channel
- Approximately one blog per month, based on the strongest validated idea

## Supported post archetypes

Choose whichever best fits the material:

1. Concise insight: hook → explanation → takeaway
2. Technical tutorial: problem → method/code → result → caveat
3. Research breakdown: claim → method → finding → limitations
4. Lesson story: mistake/event → diagnosis → lesson → practical action

## Quality bar

A post is publishable only if it is:

- Useful to a clearly identified technical reader
- Specific rather than generic
- Supported by a concrete example or code when applicable
- Supported by a credible source, statistic, benchmark, or reproducible result for external claims
- Honest about uncertainty and limitations
- Written in Pranay's direct, concise, first-person, no-hype voice
- Reviewed and approved by Pranay

## Attribution policy

- Add a compact `Sources` section with 1–3 authoritative links.
- Prefer papers, official documentation, repositories, and first-party reports.
- Look for official X and LinkedIn profiles belonging to key authors or organizations.
- Tag profiles naturally in the main post when their work is central.
- Include profile links or handles in Sources where platform conventions allow.
- Never infer or fabricate a profile handle.

## First 30-day measurement

Primary metric: publishing consistency.

```text
Consistency rate = posts published / posts scheduled
```

Secondary diagnostic metrics may be recorded but do not override consistency during the first month:

- Saves/bookmarks
- Meaningful technical comments
- Expert replies
- Profile visits
- Follower growth
- Blog clicks
